How to connect a DPP and product-registration QR flow to Shopify, Klaviyo and Gorgias

By Elvis Krastins · 25 September 2026

Short answer: a QR-driven product experience can sit alongside a commerce and support stack without pretending there is a native app. Start with a product and registration record, match a purchase only when you have a trustworthy identifier, send a consent-aware registration event to marketing, and surface the product record to support. This is an implementation pattern, not a claim that the connectors below are prebuilt or already deployed.

Disclosure: I do freelance work for Veribl, which I use as the product-experience example below. The other tools' API capabilities are linked to their own documentation. This guide was prepared with AI assistance and checked against the linked documentation.

The three jobs behind one product scan

  1. Product record: the QR code identifies a product or variant and opens the product's manuals, support and, where required, Digital Product Passport information.
  2. Owner record: registration associates that product with a buyer who chooses to identify themselves. A scan alone is not proof of ownership or consent to marketing.
  3. Commerce and support record: an order and a helpdesk customer may be separate from the new registration; match them deliberately instead of assuming one email always identifies the right order.

Veribl publicly describes a product experience with registration, interactive manuals, AI support and DPP hosting behind a QR code. Its reference page describes a REST API and HMAC-SHA256 signed webhooks for product, registration and consumer data. See Veribl's product reference. This establishes a possible integration surface, not a native Shopify, Klaviyo or Gorgias app.

Shopify: verify the order, don't assume it

Shopify's Admin GraphQL orders query supports order search filters including email, order name and confirmation number. A brand can use its own authorized middleware to look up an order when a registrant supplies the necessary information. Match on more than email when possible, confirm the product or variant is on that order, and handle gifts, retail purchases and marketplace sales as distinct paths. Someone who scans a QR code may never have a Shopify order.

Avoid presenting a registration as a verified purchase until the order match has actually succeeded. The brand should decide what evidence is sufficient for warranty activation and how to handle a manual review. The exact policy is not set by these APIs.

Klaviyo: make registration an event, not blanket consent

Klaviyo's Create Event API accepts an event with a metric name and a profile identifier such as an email address or profile ID. Middleware could send a Product Registered event with a product identifier and registration time after validating the webhook signature and deduplicating repeats. The event name and fields here are a proposed implementation, not an existing Veribl connector contract.

Registration and marketing consent are separate. Do not subscribe the owner to marketing merely because an event was sent. Map the consent state and any applicable privacy rules in the actual flow, and decide whether Veribl or Klaviyo owns each follow-up sequence so a buyer does not receive two copies.

Gorgias: show support what the customer owns

Gorgias documents an API to update customer custom-field values. A brand could store a registered-product summary or a secure link to its product record on the matching customer profile. Before implementing this, define the custom fields and an identity-match rule, and decide whether agents need a live lookup instead of copied warranty data that may go stale. The API documentation establishes the field update capability; it does not establish a turnkey Veribl-Gorgias integration.

Implementation checks

  • Verify webhook signatures before accepting registration events. Veribl says its webhooks are HMAC-SHA256 signed, but implementation details must come from the account's current developer documentation.
  • Make the event handler idempotent so retries do not create duplicate registrations, events or support records.
  • Use scoped credentials and avoid sending sensitive ownership or order data in public QR URLs.
  • Test the paths for retail, gift, marketplace and direct orders separately. A QR scan, a registration and a verified purchase are not interchangeable states.
  • Keep the passport's regulatory product data and the buyer's private registration data separated according to the applicable requirements and your privacy design.

FAQ

Does Veribl have native Shopify, Klaviyo and Gorgias apps?

This guide does not claim it does. Its public reference describes API and webhook access. The architecture above would require brand-side development or middleware, and a live app-directory check before any procurement decision.

Can a product-registration QR code also point to a Digital Product Passport?

Yes, a product experience can expose both, but the passport's public product data and an owner's private records need different access rules. Veribl describes both functions behind its QR-led experience; a brand must test the exact product and regulatory scope it needs.

What should happen after a buyer registers a product?

Validate the event, record the owner-product association, check purchase evidence if your policy requires it, then pass only the needed event or product context to marketing and support. Do not infer marketing consent from registration.
